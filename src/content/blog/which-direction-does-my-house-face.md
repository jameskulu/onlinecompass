**To find which direction your house faces, stand at the front entrance looking outward, hold your phone flat, and point its top edge straight away from the building.** Read the heading on the [online compass](/compass/): roughly 0° is north, 90° east, 180° south, and 270° west.

For a room, window, or balcony, measure outward from that particular opening instead. A house can have an east-facing entrance and a west-facing living room; both descriptions can be correct.

This guide explains how to take a repeatable reading, verify it on a map, and use the result to understand where sunlight may enter your home.

## First, decide what you are measuring

Before opening the compass, identify the surface you want to describe:

| Your question | Direction to measure |
| --- | --- |
| Which way does the front of my house face? | Straight outward from the front facade |
| Which way does my front door face? | Straight outward through the doorway |
| Which way does my room face? | Out through its main window; record other windows separately |
| Which way does my balcony face? | Straight outward from the balcony's open side |
| Which way does my garden face? | State your reference, such as looking from the house toward the far boundary |

The front door and front facade are not always aligned. A recessed entrance or a door on a side wall can point in a different direction from the street-facing wall. Record the actual feature, such as “front wall: south; entrance door: east,” rather than forcing one label onto the whole property.

## How to check house facing direction with your phone

1. **Choose your reference.** For the entrance, stand at the threshold facing outside. For a window, face directly out through the glass rather than along the wall.
2. **Open the [live compass](/compass/).** Enable the live reading and grant motion or orientation permission if requested. A dial on screen alone does not confirm that live sensor readings are available.
3. **Use true north for map comparisons.** If available, enable the true-north correction and allow location access so it can calculate local magnetic declination. Otherwise, note that the reading is magnetic.
4. **Hold the phone flat, screen upward.** Point the top edge straight outward, perpendicular to the wall. Avoid aiming diagonally toward a view or along a driveway.
5. **Check for interference.** Move away from steel doors, railings, vehicles, speakers, and magnetic accessories. If needed, step outside while keeping the phone parallel to the original outward direction.
6. **Repeat the reading.** Let it settle, note the heading, then check from another nearby position along the same alignment. If the readings differ substantially, resolve the interference before choosing a direction.

Apple's [iPhone compass guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/iphone/iph1ac0b663/ios) notes that magnetic and environmental interference can affect accuracy. A stable number beside a steel door is therefore worth checking from a clearer position.

Do not lean out of a window or over a balcony to get a reading. Use an accessible position or the map method below.

## Convert compass degrees into a facing direction

Use this eight-direction chart to describe a heading. These are equal 45° sectors centered on the familiar compass points.

| Heading in degrees | Facing direction |
| --- | --- |
| 337.5° to less than 360°, or 0° to less than 22.5° | North |
| 22.5° to less than 67.5° | Northeast |
| 67.5° to less than 112.5° | East |
| 112.5° to less than 157.5° | Southeast |
| 157.5° to less than 202.5° | South |
| 202.5° to less than 247.5° | Southwest |
| 247.5° to less than 292.5° | West |
| 292.5° to less than 337.5° | Northwest |

For example, an outward reading of **196°** falls in the south sector. Keeping the number as well as the label is useful: “south-facing, about 196° true” is more informative than “south-facing” alone.

If the reading is close to a boundary, such as 112.5°, keep the approximate degrees instead of treating a small change between “east” and “southeast” as a meaningful difference.

## Check house direction on a map without a compass

A map is a useful alternative when your phone lacks a heading sensor or the building causes magnetic interference.

1. Open a satellite or building-footprint map and locate the correct property.
2. Reset the map to **north up**. Check its compass indicator; a rotated map makes screen directions misleading.
3. Identify the wall, entrance, or window you want to measure. Satellite images may not reveal a recessed doorway or an apartment's window clearly.
4. Imagine a line perpendicular to that wall, pointing from inside the building toward the outside.
5. Compare that outward line with the map: up is north, right is east, down is south, and left is west.

For a numerical estimate, use the [bearing calculator](/bearing/) with a start point at the relevant facade and a second point directly outward along that perpendicular line. Its result describes the line between the chosen points, so inaccurate placement gives an inaccurate facade estimate. Use clearly separated points on the same alignment where possible.

**Example:** On a north-up map, your front wall runs roughly northeast to southwest. If the outside of the house is on the lower-right side of that wall, its outward direction is southeast. Measuring along the wall would give northeast or southwest instead: that is the wall's alignment, not its facing direction.

## Should you use true north or magnetic north?

Use **true north** when describing a facade relative to a north-up geographic map or checking solar direction. A magnetic compass measures against magnetic north; the angle between the two is called declination and depends on location.

The two results can differ even when both instruments work properly. Record which reference you used and compare like with like. If one phone reads magnetic north and another reads true north, recalibrating alone will not remove that difference.

Our [magnetic declination guide](/blog/magnetic-declination-explained/) explains the correction. For a basic property description, there is little value in reporting decimal-degree precision when your hand alignment and phone readings are only approximate.

## What the direction tells you about sunlight

A window's facing direction helps predict its exposure, but trees, neighboring buildings, overhangs, latitude, and the season also matter. Measure the **window**, rather than relying on the entrance direction, when planning room use or checking daylight.

- **East-facing windows** generally receive direct sun earlier in the day when the sun is visible from that side.
- **West-facing windows** generally receive direct sun later in the day and can experience afternoon glare or heat.
- **Outside the tropics in the Northern Hemisphere**, south-facing windows generally have more opportunity for direct midday sun than north-facing windows.
- **Outside the tropics in the Southern Hemisphere**, north-facing windows generally have more opportunity for direct midday sun than south-facing windows.

The [U.S. Department of Energy's daylighting overview](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-101-daylighting) discusses south-facing winter sunlight and east/west glare in the U.S. For the Southern Hemisphere perspective, [New Zealand's house orientation guide](https://www.building.govt.nz/getting-started/smarter-homes-guides/siting-and-location/house-orientation) explains planning around northern solar exposure.

Within the tropics, the midday sun can be on different sides of the sky at different times of year. Avoid applying a universal “north always gets no sun” rule. Use the [sun position tool](/sun/) for your location and the relevant date and time, then consider whether the actual window has an unobstructed view in that direction.

## Why your compass and property listing might disagree

Check these possibilities before assuming either result is wrong:

- **Different features:** The listing may describe the living room, garden, or main facade while you measured the door.
- **Opposite viewpoint:** Facing inward from the street reverses the outward heading by 180°. A 90° outward heading becomes 270° when you turn around.
- **A rotated map:** Screen-up only means north when the map is set north-up.
- **Magnetic interference:** A steel threshold, balcony railing, or magnetic case can disturb the sensor.
- **Different north references:** True and magnetic headings need the local declination correction to agree.
- **Broad labels:** “South-facing” is often an approximate description of a facade that is slightly southeast or southwest.

If readings jump or drift, follow the [phone compass calibration guide](/blog/phone-compass-calibration-fix/) and repeat the measurement away from metal. If live heading is unavailable, use the map method; allowing GPS location by itself does not supply a stationary facing direction.

## A simple record you can reuse

For an apartment viewing or a daylight check, save a note like this:

> Living-room window: approximately 240° true, southwest. Measured looking outward with the phone flat; checked against a north-up map. Neighboring building partly blocks the view.

Record each important opening separately. This gives you something useful to compare between rooms without assuming the whole home has one exposure.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I face inward or outward to check my house direction?

Face outward from the feature you are measuring. For a front door, look from inside toward outside. If you stand outside looking back at the door, you measure the opposite direction.

### Can I check my house direction without installing an app?

Yes. Use the [browser compass](/compass/) on a compatible phone with live heading access, or use a north-up map to estimate the outward direction of the facade. Browser and device sensor support vary.

### Does a north-facing front door mean every room faces north?

No. A room's exposure depends on its own windows. A corner room can have two exposures, and an apartment entrance may open into an internal corridor rather than toward daylight.

### Can I find house direction from my address alone?

An address helps locate the property, but it does not identify which facade, window, or unit you mean. Find the building on a map and select the actual outward direction you want to measure.

### Which house direction is best?

That depends on climate, hemisphere, shading, and when you use each room. Establish the window directions first, then check sunlight at the times and seasons that matter to you. A direction label alone cannot predict comfort or room brightness.

Start with the [online compass](/compass/), take two or more readings along the same outward alignment, and save the approximate degrees together with the feature you measured.
