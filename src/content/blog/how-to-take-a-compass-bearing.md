A **compass bearing** is a direction written as an angle from north. Instead of saying “walk toward the hill,” you can say “the hill is on a bearing of sixty degrees.” That makes directions precise and easy to check with a map or compass.

You can get a bearing between two places with our [bearing calculator](/bearing/), then follow it with the [online compass](/compass/). Here is how the numbers fit together and how to use them without confusing your destination's direction with the way you are currently facing.

> **In short:** Enter your starting point and destination in the bearing calculator, note the initial bearing, switch your compass to true north when comparing it with a map, then turn until your phone's heading matches the bearing.

## What does a bearing tell you?

A bearing is measured **clockwise from north**, from 0° up to (but not including) 360°:

| Bearing | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| 0° or 360° | North |
| 45° | North-east |
| 90° | East |
| 135° | South-east |
| 180° | South |
| 225° | South-west |
| 270° | West |
| 315° | North-west |

For example, a bearing of 90° means the direction is due east. A bearing of 45° points north-east. The number describes a direction from a particular starting point; it is not a direction that stays the same everywhere.

## Find the bearing between two places

The quickest way to get a bearing is to calculate it from your starting location to your destination:

1. Open the [bearing calculator](/bearing/).
2. Enter or select your starting point and destination. You can use your current location as the start if you allow location access.
3. Read the initial bearing. This is the direction to begin travelling from the starting point toward the destination.
4. Open the [compass](/compass/) and use the same north reference as the calculator.

The initial bearing gives you the direction of travel at the start of the route. On a long journey, the direction along the surface of the Earth curves slightly, so the initial bearing may not point directly at the destination from every point along the way. For a short walk, use it to orient yourself, then check your position and direction again as you go.

## Follow a bearing with your phone

For a straightforward reading, take your phone outside and hold it flat, screen facing up:

1. Enable the live compass and allow sensor access if your browser asks.
2. Move away from cars, railings, speakers, magnetic phone mounts, and other sources of magnetic interference.
3. Set the compass to **true north** if your bearing came from a map or the bearing calculator.
4. Turn your body and phone until the heading matches the bearing.
5. Pick a visible landmark in that direction and walk toward it. Check the compass again when you reach it.

The compass heading is the direction the **top edge of your phone** points. Hold it in front of you with that edge facing away, rather than turning the screen toward the destination. If the reading wanders, step away from nearby metal and magnets or trace a few slow figure-eights with the phone before trying again.

## Magnetic north or true north?

This is an important detail when you compare a compass reading to a map. Most maps use **true north**, the direction toward the geographic North Pole. A phone's magnetometer senses **magnetic north**. The angle between the two is called magnetic declination, and it varies depending on where you are.

If you use a magnetic heading against a true-north map bearing, the difference can lead you off course. Turn on the compass's **true north** setting and allow it to use your location for declination before following a map bearing. If you are using a bearing tool, make sure you understand which north reference its result uses and match the compass setting to it.

## Bearing, heading, and route are different

These terms are related but mean different things:

- **Bearing:** the direction from one location to another.
- **Heading:** the direction your phone or body is currently pointing.
- **Route:** the path you take to reach a destination.

If a destination is on a bearing of 60° but your heading is 120°, you are facing away from the direction you need to start. Turn until the compass heading is close to 60°. That points you in the right general direction, but it does not account for obstacles, private land, terrain, or turns in a trail. Use a suitable map and follow safe, accessible paths rather than walking blindly in a straight line.

## Taking a bearing from a map

On a map with a north-up grid, you can estimate a bearing by drawing a line from your current position to your destination and measuring the clockwise angle from the map's north line. A protractor can give you the angle; the [bearing calculator](/bearing/) can do the calculation from coordinates instead.

Before using the result, confirm that the map and compass use the same north reference. If the map has a grid-north reference rather than true north, follow the map's instructions for grid convergence rather than assuming the two are identical. For everyday directions, a digital bearing calculation with a true-north compass setting is usually simpler.

## When a bearing is not enough

A bearing only tells you a direction, not how far away the destination is or what lies between you and it. A straight-line bearing is not turn-by-turn navigation, and phone compass readings can be unreliable indoors or near magnetic interference.

For better results, check your location, use a map that shows paths and terrain, and take another bearing as you approach intermediate landmarks. ## A quick example

Suppose the bearing calculator says a lookout point is 70° from your current position. Set your compass to true north, hold the phone flat, and rotate until the heading reads close to 70°. The direction is a little north of north-east. Find a landmark along that line, walk to it using an appropriate path, and check the bearing again from there. The destination's bearing will change as your position changes, so recalculate when you need a fresh direction.

A bearing turns a vague direction into a number you can check. Pair it with the [bearing calculator](/bearing/) to find the direction between places and the [online compass](/compass/) to orient yourself on the ground.
